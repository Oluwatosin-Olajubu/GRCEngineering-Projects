# TechGlobal Security Governance Transformation

A consultant-style security governance redesign for **TechGlobal**, a fictional 2,500-employee technology company operating across five global offices. Produced as the Week 3 practical laboratory for **GRC102 – Information Security Governance** 

> **Course context:** GRC102, Module 3 — Roles and Responsibilities in Security Governance
> **Brief:** *Roles, Responsibilities and Accountability in Security Governance* 
> **Deliverable type:** Written consultant report + six embedded evidence bundles

---

## 1. Scenario

TechGlobal's security decisions are concentrated in the IT Director, who currently acts as security owner, risk approver, and operator all at once. There is no governance committee structure, business units make inconsistent local security decisions, and the CEO and Board have limited visibility into cyber risk.

This report, written from the perspective of an appointed **Lead Security Governance Consultant**, redesigns TechGlobal's governance model so that:

- security decisions are business-aligned,
- accountabilities are explicit and non-conflicting,
- authority conflicts are resolved by design rather than by escalation drama, and
- material cyber risk can move cleanly from operational teams to executive management and the Board.

## 2. What's in this report

The report is organised into six evidence bundles, matching the structure required by the lab brief.

| Bundle | Contents |
|---|---|
| **1 — Governance Architecture & Stakeholder Map** | Nine-item governance gap assessment, full stakeholder map (current vs. proposed authority/interest), governance organisation chart, consultant justification for the design |
| **2 — Roles, Responsibilities & Authority** | Cross-functional responsibility matrix, full role profiles (Board, CEO, CISO, CRO/Risk, Legal, Finance, HR, IT, Business Unit Leaders) with purpose, responsibilities, decision authority, reporting lines and KPIs, plus five overlapping-authority conflict analyses |
| **3 — Security Governance Committee Ecosystem** | Four-forum committee model (Board ARC, Executive Security Council, Security Governance Committee, Incident Response & Resilience Working Group), Terms of Reference for the SGC, a timed sample agenda, a four-entry sample decision log, and a 12-month governance calendar |
| **4 — RACI Accountability Matrix** | 17-activity RACI matrix (the 15 required activities plus two added for TechGlobal's risk profile), five problematic-assignment analyses, and a one-page implementation guide for managers |
| **5 — Cyber-Risk Escalation & Segregation of Duties** | Seven-step escalation workflow from detection to Board decision, a three-level escalation threshold table with named triggers and target response times |
| **6 — Segregation-of-Duties & Accountability Weakness Register** | Eight SoD/accountability weaknesses (brief requires five), each with risk created, roles involved, recommended control, and residual-risk rating; plus a decision-recording and three-lines-of-defence assurance note |

## 3. Design highlights

- **CISO independence:** the CISO is moved out of the IT reporting line and reports directly to the CEO, with a protected escalation route to the Board Audit & Risk Committee alongside the CRO and Internal Audit.
- **No new sub-board:** oversight is routed through the existing Audit & Risk Committee (quarterly cyber items, 72-hour extraordinary-meeting option) rather than creating an oversized new governance layer.
- **One standard, local voice:** a Security Champions Network gives every business unit and office a two-way channel into governance without letting anyone run a private security programme.
- **Single accountable role per activity:** the RACI matrix is checked so every governance activity has exactly one "A" — no shared or missing accountability.
- **Auditable decision trail:** every material decision is logged in a single Decision & Risk Register with owner, rationale, action, and review/expiry date, sampled by the CRO/Risk function and reviewed annually by Internal Audit.

## 5. Frameworks and standards referenced

The recommendations are grounded in recognised governance and regulatory references, including:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (clauses 5.3, 6.1, 7.5, Annex A.5.2/A.5.3)
- ISO/IEC 27014:2020 (Governance of information security)
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework — Govern function (GV.PO, GV.RM, GV.RR, GV.OV)
- NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 (AC-5, Separation of Duties)
- The IIA Three Lines Model
- GDPR Article 33 (72-hour breach notification)
- NIS2 Directive, Article 20 (governance) and Article 23 (incident reporting)
- SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules (2023)
