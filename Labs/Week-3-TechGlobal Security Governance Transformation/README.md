## Part 2 — Report Overview

**What this report is:** A governance redesign for TechGlobal (a simulated ~2,500-employee, five-office technology company) that replaces an informal model — where one IT Director owns, operates, and approves security risk — with a structured, business-aligned governance framework. Delivered as six evidence bundles required by the lab brief.

**Structure at a glance:**

| Bundle | Content | Key deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance architecture | 9 gaps, stakeholder map, org chart, justification |
| 2 | Roles & responsibilities | Responsibility matrix, 9 full role profiles, 5 conflict areas |
| 3 | Committee ecosystem | 4 governance forums, ToR, sample agenda, decision log, 12-month calendar |
| 4 | RACI matrix | 17 activities, 1 accountable role each, 5 problem assignments, implementation guide |
| 5 | Escalation workflow | 7-step workflow, 3-level threshold table |
| 6 | SoD & accountability | 8 weaknesses, corrective controls, assurance note |

**Core design decisions used in this project:**
- The CISO is moved out of the IT reporting line and reports to the CEO, with direct Board access — this is the fix for the central conflict (Gap G1) running through the whole report.
- Risk *assessment* and risk *acceptance* are always split between different roles — no one accepts a risk they assessed alone.
- Four governance forums (Board committee, Executive Security Council, Security Governance Committee, Incident Response & Resilience Working Group) plus a Champions Network give BUs a voice without letting them run separate security programmes.
- Escalation levels are triggered by objective thresholds (loss amount, record count, outage length), not individual judgement — this removes the self-classification bias flagged in Bundle 4.

**References** are listed at the end of the report (ISO 27001, ISO 27014, NIST CSF, GDPR Art. 33, NIS2, IIA Three Lines Model).
